## Review boundary and rejected work

The installed benchmark runtime SHA-256 remains `481eab6a7fca0d8c22aef03152bd685d84cdbe7369e7ffa3e0e9846de45b43f4`, matching the archived affected-warm-start runtime. The colored symmetric-sweep experiment has now been tested and **reverted**. Its corrected implementation passed numerical/memory and frozen penetration checks, but increased complete-step cost in matched bombardment screens. No experimental preconditioner remains in the production source. [Generated comparison and rejected patch](/root/workspace/physx-2/qualification/colored-sweep-evaluation/report.html).

**Three distinct skip mechanisms:**

- ✅ **Within a solve:** each exact bond-connected stress component retires when its own residual passes. This still requires preparation and residual evaluation; zero iterations is not zero work.
- ✅ **Across topology updates:** unchanged generation bypasses partition reconstruction. When another component fractures, unaffected bond warm starts survive, but hierarchy/inverse invalidation remains too broad.
- ⬜ **Across timesteps:** general settled-island solve reuse is missing from the integrated entry point. `solveDeviceAsync` rejects `skipSettledIslands` and `skipStableUnconverged`. The old implementation includes input/settings comparison and skip masks, with host planning/upload work that should be replaced by device-owned validation.

Stress components describe structural equations; rigid clusters describe shared motion; contact/constraint islands describe coupled collision response. They are not interchangeable scheduling partitions. Rigid-body sleeping also does not, by itself, prove a stress/damage result reusable.

The source's default iteration setting is a **per-frame maximum** with warm starts. The native path currently rejects incomplete within-step solves. This is an existing behavioral difference (S06), not a ported optimization or proof that the old policy was wrong.

## What matters most for the 256-building bombardment

Workload: **256 buildings, 113,664 chunks, 229,376 bonds, 256 projectiles; 3 simulated seconds / 180 steps; maximum one correction; sleeping disabled**. At reference peak step 103 the complete advance was **62.035 ms**. A separate intrusive replay counted **1,606 stress components** and **94,421 component updates** before fracture; these are numerical stress components, not the active rigid-body count.

In that diagnostic replay, **256 retained components above 256 iterations account for 99.9594% of live operator visits**. **15.61% of CSR visits reject dead entries**. These are work counts, not time-saving predictions or proof of memory/compute saturation. Diagnostic wall times are not production timings. Physical/convergence counters matched the reference; one step had a one-update iteration-count difference. Counter agreement does not prove identical trajectories.
